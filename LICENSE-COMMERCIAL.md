## Which one applies to you

This section is a plain-language summary to help you work out which licence you
need. It is not legal advice and it does not modify either licence — where this
summary and the AGPL disagree, the AGPL text governs.

**The AGPL is very likely fine if** you are evaluating InlaySQL, using it for
personal or internal purposes, doing research or teaching with it, or building
something you are willing to release under the AGPL yourself.

**You probably want a commercial licence if** you want to embed InlaySQL in a
product you distribute under terms of your own, or offer a service over a
network built on it, without releasing your own source under the AGPL.

The specific thing that distinguishes the AGPL from the ordinary GPL is
**section 13**: if users interact with your modified version *over a network*,
you must offer those users the corresponding source. That reaches software you
never distribute as a binary — which for an embedded database is exactly the
case worth thinking about before you adopt it.

If you are unsure which side of the line you are on, ask us rather than guess.
We would rather answer the question than have anyone find out later.

## Contributing

Contributions are welcome, and there is something you should know before you
send one. Dual licensing only works if one party can license the whole work
under both licences, so we ask contributors to agree that their contribution may
be released under **both** the AGPL and a commercial licence. Practically: open
the pull request, and we will raise this before merging anything substantial.

If you would rather your contribution stayed AGPL-only, say so in the pull
request. That is a legitimate position and we will tell you honestly whether we
can take the change on those terms rather than merging it and sorting it out
later.
